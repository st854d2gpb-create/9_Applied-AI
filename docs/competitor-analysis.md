# Competitor analysis: Baumarkt Buddy

*Market research for the MBA Applied AI group project · as of 31 October 2026*

> **Method:** desk research from public sources (company announcements, trade press, app pages). The tools were not tested hands-on, and some articles were only partly readable (paywall). Capabilities are described as the companies advertise them.

## Summary

- The big US chains (Home Depot, Lowe's) already run full AI shopping assistants. Baumarkt Buddy should **not** be pitched as "the first AI DIY assistant".
- German chains have only **partial tools**: OBI has in-store navigation and human chat, Hornbach has an AI quantity calculator, and Bauhaus has an AI advisor for flooring only.
- In our sources, no German chain combines everything: project → checked shopping list → aisle and shelf → step-by-step guide → safety checks.
- **Safety is our clearest point of difference.** None of the competitor material we found stresses catalogue-only answers or safety stops.

**Why it matters for the pitch:** in a shrinking market, chains fight each other for share. A tool that brings customers in and makes their baskets bigger is easier to sell to them.

## 2. Direct competitors

| Competitor | What they offer | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Home Depot (US): Magic Apron** | AI project advice in plain language, suggestions for related products, and in-store maps down to the aisle and bay with live stock. A materials-list builder for pros. Built on Google Gemini. | Big-tech partnership. Covers the whole journey from "how to" to "done". Serves both DIY customers and pros. Plans to appear inside Google's AI Mode and the Gemini app. |
| **Lowe's (US): Mylow** | AI assistant handling about **1 million questions a month**. Mylow Companion helps store staff. Step-by-step guides, product bundles, Spanish voice and text. | Uses AI for customers *and* staff. Pro orders that took hours are prepared in minutes. Store visits by leadership feed back into the model. |
| **Kingfisher (B&Q, Castorama)** | AI DIY assistant on Castorama since 2023: project advice and product tips, 24/7. In March 2026 it announced AI shopping agents with Google Cloud for the UK, France and Poland. | Moving from keyword search to conversation: plan the project, build the list, buy. |
| **OBI: heyOBI app** | Markt-Navi in-store finder, shopping list, stock check. Free advice from human OBI experts by message. 1% discount, coupons, €500 project bonus. **No AI is advertised.** | Loyalty-led: discounts and bonuses keep customers in the app. Human experts instead of AI. |
| **Hornbach: Macher-Assistent** | AI that calculates material amounts, for example laminate including pack sizes and waste. Uses Hornbach's own data. | Presents AI as an *extension* of in-store advice, not a replacement. |
| **Bauhaus: PIA** | AI product advisor in the online shop, live by October 2026, for **flooring only** so far. | Starts with one narrow, high-value category. |
| **toom** | 24/7 chat answered by trained "brand advocates" (GUURU) plus a bot. Answers in 28 seconds on average, about 13,200 requests a month, rated 4.4/5. | Community and humans for advice. AI is used behind the scenes, for example for pricing. |

## 3. Indirect competitors

- **IKEA** put an AI assistant in the OpenAI GPT Store in 2024. Its strategy is to be present where customers already chat.
- **Independent AI apps** such as AskForeman plan DIY projects from text or photos, but they aren't linked to a store's real products or aisles.
- **ChatGPT, YouTube and how-to blogs** are free and well known, but they can't tell a customer what a specific store stocks or where to find it.

## 5. What this means for Baumarkt Buddy

1. **Don't call it "the first AI DIY assistant".** Home Depot and Lowe's already run them at scale.
2. **Germany has a gap.** No German chain in our sources covers the full journey; each covers only one piece.
3. **Lead with safety and trust.** Baumarkt Buddy recommends only catalogue products, stops when the wall or load is unknown, and warns customers to hire a professional.
4. **Business model: white-label software for mid-sized chains** such as toom, hagebau or Globus. They lack Home Depot's budget for their own AI. A staff mode (like Mylow Companion) could be an extra selling point.
5. **Learn from competitors:**
   - Loyalty keeps people in the app (OBI): link the shopping list to coupons or bonus points.
   - Start narrow (Bauhaus): a few projects done well beats many done badly.
   - Position AI as help for staff, not a replacement (Hornbach, Lowe's).
