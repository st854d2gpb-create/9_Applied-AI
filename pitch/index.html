06UML · sequence diagram
~1:30Baumarkt-Buddy

One request, step by step: hang an 80 cm shelf

Hang a shelf · D-13Click to build the diagram

8 steps, from the first sentence to the checked list.

1Six players, one request

The customer and the app in the browser. Filter, parser and rule engine are plain code. Products and plug ratings come from our Excel catalogue. No AI model decides.

2Describe, safety first

The customer types or speaks the project. Before anything else, filter G0 looks for high-risk work: electrics, gas, load-bearing walls. Here nothing is found.

3Understand: only guesses

The keyword parser reads project, wall and length from the sentence. These are only guesses; nothing is decided yet.

4The customer confirms

Every guess is shown as "guess – please confirm". Nothing unconfirmed reaches the rule engine (SR-01).

5Two short questions

Screen 1: wall type. Screen 2: size plus the used-parts check. A defect in an old board or bracket means: buy new (ST-06).

6The rule engine plans

Board and brackets follow the size. Each plug's rating comes from sheet System ratings. The weakest part decides, safety factor 1.25 included, depth checked against the wall.

7The customer chooses the load

The app shows what each plug holds: 6 mm up to 20 kg … 12 mm up to 50 kg. The customer picks one and confirms "I will load max. 32 kg".

8List or stop

All checks pass: checked list with aisle, shelf and evidence log. Wall unknown or shelf too deep: no list, rule ID shown, ask a store associate.

The process is written in markdown
1
Team sets requirements, catalogue, safety rules and decisions D-1 to D-14
2
Agent codes on its own branch, never on main. Each commit names its prompt (CLAUDE.md)
3
CI runs all 128 tests on every pull request. Red means not done
4
Human gate reviews and merges. App code and AI proxy need owner approval (CODEOWNERS)
Where the AI got it wrong, and what we changed
  • ✗Right stop, wrong reason: a 100 cm shelf with 10 kg was blocked "for load"; the cause was board length. Found in team testing; message now names the real cause
  • ✗Typos: "Regla" was not understood. Fix applied to the safety filter too, so "Steckdoze" is still refused
  • ✗Missing data: the agent filled in wall types itself. Marked "team to verify" instead of silently trusted
The AI saved us days. The decisions and the responsibility stayed with us.
10Key takeaways & lessons learned
~2:00Baumarkt-Buddy

What we built, and what we learned building it

Key takeaways

The product
< 2 minidea to list
One integrated app, end to end

Describe the project, answer the questions, get the checked shopping list in under two minutes.

Rulesnot LLM decisions
Rule-based decisions instead of LLM judgement

Safety: no hallucinated products, loads or fixings. Security: no LLM decision harness or API exposed to constant attack.

100 %traceable
Every choice is explained

The customer can read the evidence log and see why each item is on the final list.

Lessons learned

The process
1
Process makes AI coding reliable

Following the process is as important as the execution itself.

2
AI is fast, but only as good as its instructions

Clear instructions and explicit off-limits prevent hallucination.

3
Safety must be deterministic

The model suggests. Code checks catalogue, fit and load.

The AI proposes. Deterministic code decides. People stay accountable.
